I use AI to proofread my blog posts before publishing. I know this is a controversial thing to say on the small/indie web, but I do. This has always been clearly stated on my [[../ai|AI policy page]] since the inception of this website. What I said there was honest but not enough. Proofreading is a fuzzy term. It might mean simply fixing typos in the text, making editorial suggestions, or even rephrasing and finishing a draft. Where do you draw the line?

Where do *I* draw the line?

A few weeks ago, I was, once again, complaining about artificial intelligence in my weekly psychoanalysis session: "It writes code faster and better; I've never felt this alienated about my work." The analyst then asked: what if AI could sculpt faster and better than me? Would I feel the same frustration? "No", I answered, "I wouldn't even care if AI could sculpt [The Veiled Virgin](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/The_Veiled_Virgin) in an hour." I don't sculpt to produce sculptures as effectively as possible. Heck, I don't even sculpt to produce sculptures as perfectly as possible. I sculpt because I like it. I like the process, I like the frustration, and I like the feeling when I finally get the form the way it's supposed to be.[^1]

I feel the same way about my writing. I don't write to bring posts into existence. I write because I want to write. I know it's very easy to prompt something like "write an essay about what art is, juxtaposing the ideas of Arthur Danto and Susan Sontag and artworks of Marcel Duchamp and Maurizio Cattelan" and make Claude slop it through in a few minutes. But what's the point? This essay is waiting in my drafts folder for me to figure out what I really want to say. When I finally finish it, it'll be in my own words and my own thinking.

So, why do I use AI to proofread my writing? Because I'm ashamed of my broken grammar. I make a lot of silly mistakes: I keep using past tense where I should use past perfect, keep forgetting articles, and use awkward phrasing instead of idiomatic English. I care about these mistakes. I want to present myself as an intelligent person. You can call me vain for caring about how I present myself, but I won't let you call me dishonest.

If you check [[./"I want a president"|my]] [[./Chasing art in New York|last]] [[./Finally back home|four]] [[./Tetsu Kesuya's 10-pours v60 recipe|posts]], you'll see a link at the bottom: "Proofread with AI · see edits". There you can see the diff between my final draft and the published version. You can also see each grammar fix AI made to my writing and any suggestions it made for readability and coherence. Some of its suggestions I address *myself*, and some I simply ignore. You can see what I addressed and what I didn't. I hope this provides more transparency about the extent to which AI was used on words I've written.


[^1]: A good follow-up question would be why I don't feel like this when it comes to programming. Sadly, the analyst didn't ask it in the session. But I'd like to meditate on it in another blog post.
