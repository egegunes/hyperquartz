Many of these generalize beyond pottery.

- clay
	- different properties of the clay means your technique needs to change
		- porcelain needs less water, you throw with slip instead of water
	- preparing the clay well matters otherwise you're fighting a losing battle from the onset
		- bubbles, uneven reclaim consistency, wedging direction (clockwise or counterclockwise should generally match wheel direction)
	- clay can get tired if you throw and reclaim it too many times
		- you lose the tiny particles that give it plasticity
		- recycle throwing water and throwing slip
		- ironically, mold is not always bad
			- it helps breakdown clay particles and returns some of the plasticity to tired clay
- routine and showing up
	- routine builds community -- it is a third space
		- show up at the same time and same place continously and the people who also do become familiar faces
	- [[thoughts/50 pounds of pots|50 pounds of pots]]
		- volume matters
		- and you can only achieve volume by showing up
- on being attached to your work
	- everything can be saved but it's not always worth the effort to
		- wall becoming too thing when pulling, bisque fix, reglazing
		- learning to kill you darlings and try again
			- see volume argument above
	- despite your best efforts, you're not going to know what the glaze looks like until you fire it
		- small variations in glaze mixtures, kiln placement, humidity, clay body, etc. all matter
- technique
	- there are step function changes in forms that require changes in technique to throw properly
		- bowls after a certain size centrifugal force matters
		- after a certain thinness on curved forms, curvature matters a lot to keep it stable
		- cylinders after a certain height you can no longer couple your hands so you need to develop better proprioception
			- after even more, you cannot even see your hands as they are inside the cylinder
	- there is no 'right' way to do anything
		- everyone has different bodies and different goals for what they are trying to make. you can take inspiration from others but ultimately find what works for your body.
	- you can't learn everything just by watching
		- so much tactility / fluency is learned by doing and getting reps in
	- getting better at pottery is less about getting stronger to control the clay better but more about the tactile fluency with the material itself and with your own body
		- being in dialogue with the clay, knowing when you can push it further, when its tired, knowing how much clay you have left in the walls, hearing the sound of clay thats just about too thin to trim any more
			- cant collar too aggressively, cant belly out too aggressively, cant pull too aggressively
		- being in dialogue with your body, knowing where your hands are when you can't see them, knowing if your elbow is pointed up to execute a straight pull, knowing how much pressure to apply if at all
