Note: these are not all about pottery.

- its very important to process your clay well before throwing with it
- everything can be recycled and saved but it's not always worth the effort to
- getting better at pottery is less about getting stronger to control the clay better but more about the tactile fluency with the material itself
	- being in dialogue with the clay, knowing when you can push it further, when its tired, knowing how much clay you have left in the walls or base
- you can't learn everything just by watching
	- so much tactility / fluency is learned by doing and getting reps in
- there is no 'right' way to do anything
	- everyone has different bodies and different goals for what they are trying to make. you can take inspiration from others but ultimately find what works for your body.
- despite your best efforts, you're not going to know what the glaze looks like until you fire it
	- small variations in glaze mixtures, kiln placement, humidity, clay body, etc. all matter
- there are step function changes in clay weight that require changes in technique to throw properly
