I'm finally back home after 18 days of schlepping[^1] through the United States of America. I spent 10 days in New York City with my wife, 6 days in Salt Lake City, Utah, and wasted the last 2 days in airports due to [the mysterious FAA ground stop](https://www.faa.gov/newsroom/nationwide-ground-stop-questions-and-answers).

Days in NYC were magical. I'd never felt this at home in a foreign city before visiting New York. [[I’ve walked these streets before|The déjà vu]] that enchanted my experience right after arriving in the city played a big part in this magical feeling. Another reason was that my negative biases against the United States and NYC were shattered quickly.

First and foremost, I thought the people of NYC would be rude and the streets of New York would be somehow dangerous. Both were far from reality. I was surprised how polite and friendly every single person we interacted with was. I also didn't feel unsafe at any given moment (although I felt the need to stay alert on some occasions), even though we were staying in a place some might call "sketchy".

We stayed in Queensbridge, Long Island City. Queensbridge is the home of [the largest housing project](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Queensbridge_Houses) in North America. It's a neighborhood of many black and latino families and the hood of [Nas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Nas) and [Mobb Deep](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Mobb_Deep). I enjoyed staying in this place because it felt more real than the fancy streets of Manhattan. The hood was full of people on the streets all day long and we were never threatened by anyone. Ironically, I was mocked, harassed and once even stalked for a long time in Europe and this was never the case in NYC.

New York delivered on multiple fronts. I was excited to see its famous art museums and [[./Chasing art in New York|I really enjoyed our visits]]. Food was great too, even though I found pizzas overrated (I'm not a pizza fan tho). To my surprise, the coffee was decent almost everywhere. Usually I'm forced to seek out specialty coffee shops while I'm traveling. I've never felt this need in the US. Regular drip coffee was a-okay.

Another highlight of the NYC trip was Broadway. We had the chance to see Wicked at the Gershwin Theatre. We got the tickets very cheap thanks to the rush lottery, although our seats had restricted views. As usual, my amazing wife did whatever she could to elevate our experience. She checked the empty seats just before the show started and we moved to one of the best spots in the venue. It was a 10/10 experience.

After 10 days in New York, my wife returned home and I went to Salt Lake City, Utah for KubeCon. SLC was a fundamentally different experience than NYC. It was almost impossible to walk anywhere and public transit was mostly useless for my destinations. Nevertheless, I'm grateful for that trip as well because I got to experience a regular American city. It was all about KubeCon rather than the city anyway. And the event was great (although very tiring)! My company was a sponsor and we had a booth, so 99% of my time was at the booth talking with people. It was fun to meet and talk with our users and our contributors.

On my way back, I didn't sleep much on the planes. I slept maybe 3 hours in 30 hours. I thought I had bypassed the jet lag. I did not. Yesterday was almost like a dream and I crashed hard during the day. Today is the first day I feel like I'm alive again.

[^1]: A long and tiresome walk. Yiddish, New York slang.
